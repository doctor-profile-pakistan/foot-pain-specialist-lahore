What a nerve block does

Not all foot pain comes from a tendon or a joint. Some of it is carried by a nerve.

When a nerve is irritated or compressed, the pain it produces often does not match the pattern of a tendon or joint problem. It may be burning rather than aching, it may spread beyond the foot, and it may be accompanied by numbness or tingling.

A block interrupts that signal temporarily. If the pain disappears while the block is working, the nerve is confirmed as the source. That is useful information, because it changes what should be treated next.

The same procedure can also be given with the intention of relief rather than diagnosis. Which of the two it is will be clear before you consent to it.

Two purposes

The procedure is the same. What it is being used for is not.

Diagnostic block

Confirming the source

Used where the examination points to more than one possible structure, or where imaging has not settled the question. If the pain lifts while the nerve is blocked, that nerve is responsible.

The information is what matters here, not the duration of relief.

Therapeutic block

Reducing the pain

Used where the nerve is already identified as the source, and the aim is to settle the pain for an extended period rather than to test it.

How long the relief lasts varies with the medication and with the condition, and is discussed with you beforehand.

When a block is used for foot pain

A block is considered when the pain behaves like nerve pain rather than tissue pain.

  • Burning painA nerve is more likely than a tendon
  • Electric or shootingTravels along a nerve path
  • NumbnessChange in sensation rather than pain alone
  • Nerve entrapmentPressure on a nerve around the ankle, such as tarsal tunnel syndrome
  • Diabetes-relatedNerve pain in the feet from a systemic cause
  • Unclear sourceWhere examination and imaging do not agree

Nerve entrapment around the ankle and nerve pain in the feet caused by diabetes are both assessed at this clinic. Suitability for a block is decided after the examination, not in advance.

What happens during a nerve block

The appointment is completed in the clinic on an outpatient basis.

  1. Assessment. The history and examination identify which nerve is suspected, and the skin is checked.

  2. Positioning. The foot or ankle is positioned so the nerve can be seen on ultrasound or C-Arm.

  3. Placement. The needle is advanced under guidance until it sits around the target nerve.

  4. Injection. The medication is given once the position is confirmed.

  5. Observation. You are observed afterwards, and what changed is recorded. That record is part of the diagnostic value.

What a block can and cannot do

An honest account of the limits, so you can decide with clear expectations.

A block interrupts a pain signal. It does not remove the cause of the irritation, and it does not repair a damaged nerve.

Relief is usually temporary. That is acceptable where the purpose is diagnosis. Where the purpose is relief, the duration varies, and a repeat block may be needed.

It is not a substitute for treating an underlying problem, and it is not a substitute for surgery where surgery is genuinely required.

Not every case requires a procedure. If rest, a change of footwear, or physiotherapy is the appropriate treatment, you will be advised accordingly.

Questions about nerve blocks

For anything not covered here, call the clinic.

What is a nerve block?

A nerve block places medication around a specific nerve to interrupt the pain signals it carries. It is used both to identify which nerve is responsible and to reduce the pain it is causing.

What is the difference between a diagnostic and a therapeutic block?

A diagnostic block is used to confirm whether a particular nerve is the source of the pain. A therapeutic block is given for relief. The same procedure can serve both purposes.

How long does the relief last?

It varies with the medication used and with the condition being treated. Some blocks settle the pain for a short period, others for longer. What to expect in your case is discussed before the procedure.

Are nerve blocks used for foot pain?

Yes, where the pain is carried by a nerve rather than coming from a tendon or joint. Nerve-related pain in the foot and ankle is assessed at this clinic, including nerve entrapment around the ankle.

Is a nerve block the same as an epidural?

No. An epidural is placed in the spine. A peripheral nerve block is placed around a specific nerve away from the spine, which is what is used for foot and ankle pain.
