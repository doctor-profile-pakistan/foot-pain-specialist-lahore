Why foot pain keeps returning

Rest helps almost any foot pain for a while. It helps for a much shorter time when the tissue underneath has not healed.

Most foot pain improves on its own. A change of shoes, a few days of rest, or a short course of painkillers is often enough for the pain to settle.

It returns when the underlying cause has not been treated. The pain usually comes back in the same place, and it is often worse with the first steps in the morning or after sitting for a while.

The structures under and around the foot carry several times your body weight with every step. When one of them becomes inflamed, worn, or compressed, rest helps for a short period and then stops helping. A proper assessment identifies which structure is responsible, so treatment can be directed at that structure rather than at the pain alone.

Image-guided injections

Ultrasound · C-Arm fluoroscopy

Medication is placed precisely into the joint, tendon or fascia using ultrasound or C-Arm fluoroscopy. Precise placement determines whether an injection resolves the problem or provides relief for only a few weeks.

PRP and regenerative medicine

Platelet-rich plasma · growth factors · exosomes

Platelet-rich plasma is prepared from a sample of your own blood, concentrated, and injected into damaged tissue to support healing. Growth factor therapy and exosome therapy follow the same principle.

Nerve blocks

Diagnostic · therapeutic

Where pain is carried by a specific nerve, a block confirms the source of the pain and can reduce it for an extended period. Diagnostic and therapeutic blocks are performed under image guidance.

When to be assessed

Some foot pain settles on its own. These signs mean it should be looked at.

  1. The pain has lasted more than a few weeks, or keeps returning after rest.

  2. Painkillers, footwear changes or physiotherapy have stopped helping.

  3. The pain wakes you at night, or is present at rest.

  4. Walking, standing or climbing stairs has become difficult.

  5. There is swelling, numbness or a change in how the foot feels.

Questions about foot pain

For anything not covered here, call the clinic.

When should foot pain be assessed by a doctor?

Foot pain should be assessed when it has lasted more than a few weeks, keeps returning after rest, or has stopped responding to painkillers, footwear changes or physiotherapy. Pain that wakes you at night or limits walking is a reason to be seen sooner.

Which foot conditions are treated at this clinic?

Plantar fasciitis, heel pain, Achilles tendinopathy, ankle joint pain, ankle sprains, chronic ankle instability, tarsal tunnel syndrome and nerve pain in the feet related to diabetes.

Is foot pain treatment in Lahore available without surgery?

Yes. Treatment is provided through image-guided injections, nerve blocks and regenerative medicine such as platelet-rich plasma. All procedures are minimally invasive and performed on an outpatient basis.

Do I need an MRI before the consultation?

No. An MRI is not required to book. If you already have one, or any X-rays or previous reports, bring them with you. Further imaging is requested only where the assessment indicates it is needed.
