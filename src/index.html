When foot pain keeps coming back

Most foot pain improves on its own. A change of shoes, a few days of rest, or a short course of painkillers is often enough for the pain to settle.

It returns when the underlying cause has not been treated. The pain usually comes back in the same place, and it is often worse with the first steps in the morning or after sitting for a while.

The structures under and around the foot carry several times your body weight with every step. When one of them becomes inflamed, worn, or compressed, rest helps for a short period and then stops helping. A proper assessment identifies which structure is responsible, so treatment can be directed at that structure rather than at the pain alone.

Non-surgical foot pain treatment

Regenerative medicine supports the body's own repair process in damaged tissue. Image guidance allows the physician to place treatment at the specific structure responsible for the pain, rather than in the general area around it. Every procedure offered here is minimally invasive, image-guided, and performed on an outpatient basis without surgery.

  • Minimally invasive
  • Ultrasound & C-Arm guided
  • Outpatient
  • No surgery

  1. Image-guided injections

    Ultrasound and C-Arm fluoroscopy are used to place medication precisely into the joint, tendon or fascia. Precise placement is the difference between an injection that resolves the problem and one that provides relief for only a few weeks.

    Image-guided injections →

  2. Nerve blocks

    Where pain is carried by a specific nerve, a block confirms the source of the pain and can reduce it for an extended period. Diagnostic and therapeutic blocks are performed under image guidance.

    Nerve blocks →

  3. PRP and regenerative medicine

    Platelet-rich plasma is prepared from a sample of your own blood, concentrated, and injected into damaged tissue to support healing. Growth factor therapy and exosome therapy follow the same principle: they support tissue repair rather than mask the pain.

    PRP for foot pain →

Not every case requires a procedure. If rest, a change of footwear, or physiotherapy is the appropriate treatment, you will be advised accordingly at the consultation.

Foot and ankle conditions treated here

Pain arising from the soft tissue and joints of the foot, assessed and treated by a pain physician.

  • Plantar fasciitis → Sharp heel pain, worst on the first steps of the day
  • Heel pain → Pain under the heel that builds through the day
  • Achilles tendinopathy → Pain and stiffness at the back of the ankle
  • Ankle joint pain → Arthritis, old sprains and ongoing instability

Questions patients ask

If your question is not here, call the clinic directly.

Who should see a foot pain specialist?

You should be assessed if foot, heel or ankle pain has lasted more than a few weeks, keeps returning after rest, or has stopped responding to painkillers, footwear changes or physiotherapy. Pain that wakes you at night or limits walking is also a clear reason to seek assessment.

Do I need a referral to book an appointment?

No referral is required. Call 0300-8444363 to book directly. If you already have X-rays, an MRI or previous reports, bring them to the first consultation.

What happens at the first consultation?

The consultation begins with a history of the pain and a physical examination of the foot and ankle. Where it is useful, ultrasound is used in the clinic to look at the tendon, fascia or joint directly. A treatment plan is then discussed with you, including the option of no procedure at all.

Is PRP available for foot pain?

Yes. Platelet-rich plasma is prepared from your own blood and injected into the damaged tissue under image guidance. It is used for plantar fasciitis, Achilles tendinopathy and joint-related pain. Suitability is decided at the consultation.

What kind of practice is this?

This is an interventional pain medicine practice. Foot and ankle pain is treated through image-guided injections, nerve blocks and regenerative medicine, without surgery.
