Ankle Pain Treatment in Lahore

Ankle pain usually comes from the joint itself, from a ligament that healed badly, or from the tendon behind the ankle. Each is assessed differently and treated differently, and none of it requires surgery here.

Three common patterns

Most ankle pain falls into one of these, and the history usually separates them before any imaging.

Ankle arthritis. The cartilage lining the joint wears down, and the joint becomes stiff and painful, particularly in the morning or after a period of rest. It often follows an old injury that damaged the joint surface years earlier.

An unresolved sprain. The ligament healed but the joint did not settle. Pain, swelling or a feeling of weakness persists long after the original injury, which is a different problem from a fresh sprain.

Chronic instability. Repeated sprains stretch the ligaments that hold the ankle. The ankle then gives way on uneven ground, and each episode adds further strain to the joint.

Pain can also be referred from the Achilles tendon at the back of the ankle, or carried by a nerve. Those possibilities are examined at the consultation rather than assumed.

What it feels like

Where the pain sits within the ankle narrows the cause considerably.

  • Deep in the jointAching pain, stiffness, worse in the morning
  • Front of the anklePain on bending the foot upwards, as in stairs
  • Outer sideSuggests old ligament injury or instability
  • Back of the anklePoints towards the Achilles tendon
  • Giving wayInstability rather than pain alone
  • Burning or tinglingSuggests a nerve is involved

Why it happens

Previous injury is the single most common reason.

An ankle sprain that was not rehabilitated leaves the joint less stable and the ligaments less able to protect it. Over years, that shows up as arthritis or as repeated giving way.

Load matters as well. Long periods on the feet, a rise in body weight, or a change in footwear all increase the force passing through the joint with every step.

Age contributes, but ankle arthritis is more often post-traumatic than simply age-related. That is why the history of previous sprains is asked about in detail.

How ankle pain is treated here

Treatment is directed at the joint, the ligament or the nerve, depending on what the assessment finds.

  • Non-surgical
  • Ultrasound & C-Arm guided
  • Outpatient
  • No surgery

Load and footwear

First line

The first step is reducing the force passing through the joint and supporting the ankle so it is not under constant strain. Physiotherapy is arranged where it is appropriate.

If this is the right treatment for your case, you will be advised to follow it rather than being offered an injection.

Image-guided joint injection

Ultrasound or C-Arm guided

Medication is placed inside the ankle joint under ultrasound or C-Arm guidance. Entering the joint accurately matters more here than in most places, because the space is narrow and surrounded by structures that should not be injected.

Platelet-rich plasma

Regenerative

Platelet-rich plasma is prepared from a sample of your own blood and injected into the joint or around a damaged ligament to support the repair response. It is used where the problem has been present for longer, or where an earlier injection gave only short-lived relief.

Nerve block

Where the pain is nerve-related

If the examination suggests a nerve is carrying the pain, a block confirms it and can reduce the pain for an extended period. This separates nerve pain from joint pain when the two are difficult to tell apart.

When to be assessed

These signs mean the ankle should be looked at rather than waited out.

  1. The pain has lasted more than six weeks, or keeps returning after rest.

  2. The ankle gives way on uneven ground, or has sprained repeatedly.

  3. It is swollen, stiff in the morning, or painful at rest.

  4. You cannot put weight on it after an injury.

  5. There is numbness, burning or tingling around the ankle.

An ankle that cannot bear weight immediately after an injury should be assessed urgently rather than at a routine clinic appointment.

Questions about ankle pain

For anything not covered here, call the clinic.

What causes ankle pain?

The most common causes are arthritis of the ankle joint, an old sprain that never settled, and chronic instability after repeated sprains. Pain can also be referred from a nerve or from the tendon behind the ankle.

Can ankle arthritis be treated without surgery?

Yes. Treatment here is non-surgical and includes image-guided joint injections and platelet-rich plasma, alongside load and footwear advice. Where the joint has advanced structural damage, that is discussed openly at the consultation.

Do old ankle sprains need treatment?

Not always. A sprain that healed fully needs nothing further. One that left persistent pain, swelling or a feeling that the ankle gives way should be assessed, because ongoing instability can lead to further damage over time.

Is PRP used for ankle pain?

Yes. Platelet-rich plasma prepared from your own blood can be injected into the ankle joint or around a damaged ligament under image guidance. Suitability is decided at the consultation.
