Treatment options

Three approaches, used alone or in combination depending on what the assessment finds.

  • Minimally invasive
  • Ultrasound & C-Arm guided
  • Outpatient
  • No surgery

PRP and regenerative medicine

Platelet-rich plasma · growth factors · exosomes

Platelet-rich plasma is prepared from a sample of your own blood, concentrated, and injected into damaged tissue to support healing. Growth factor therapy and exosome therapy follow the same principle: they support tissue repair rather than mask the pain.

Used for plantar fasciitis, Achilles tendinopathy and ankle joint pain.

Image-guided injections

Ultrasound · C-Arm fluoroscopy

Medication is placed precisely into the joint, tendon or fascia using ultrasound or C-Arm fluoroscopy. Precise placement determines whether an injection resolves the problem or provides relief for only a few weeks.

Used for joint pain, tendon pain and inflammation around the ankle and foot.

Image-guided injections →

Nerve blocks

Diagnostic · therapeutic

Where pain is carried by a specific nerve, a block confirms the source of the pain and can reduce it for an extended period. Diagnostic and therapeutic blocks are performed under image guidance.

Used where the source of pain is not clear from examination alone, or where nerve-related pain has not settled.

How treatment is decided

The assessment comes first. The procedure, if one is needed, follows from it.

  1. History. When the pain started, where it sits, what makes it better or worse, and what has already been tried.

  2. Examination. The foot and ankle are examined directly. Ultrasound is used in the clinic where it helps to see the tendon, fascia or joint.

  3. Plan. The options are discussed with you, including rest, footwear change, physiotherapy, or no procedure at all.

  4. Procedure. Where an injection is appropriate, it is performed in the clinic under image guidance.

Conditions these treatments address

Full detail on each condition, including how it is assessed.

  • Plantar fasciitis → Heel pain that is worst on the first steps of the day
  • Heel pain → Pain under the heel that builds through the day
  • Achilles tendinopathy → Pain and stiffness at the back of the ankle
  • Ankle joint pain → Arthritis, old sprains and ongoing instability

Questions about treatment

For anything not covered here, call the clinic.

Do I need a referral for foot pain treatment?

No referral is required. You can book directly by calling 0300-8444363. If you already have X-rays, an MRI or previous reports, bring them to the first consultation.

Is any of this treatment surgical?

No. Every option listed here is minimally invasive and performed on an outpatient basis without surgery.

How is the right treatment chosen?

Treatment is chosen after an assessment of your history and a physical examination of the foot or ankle, with ultrasound used in the clinic where it helps. The plan is then discussed with you, including the option of no procedure at all.
